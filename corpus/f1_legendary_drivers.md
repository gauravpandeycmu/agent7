# Legendary Formula One Drivers

## Ayrton Senna (Brazil)

Three-time World Champion (**1988, 1990, 1991**). Senna's wet-weather mastery at Monaco **1984** and Donington **1993** remains legendary. His rivalry with Alain Prost defined McLaren-Honda's peak. Senna died at Imola **1994**, accelerating the sport's safety push.

## Michael Schumacher (Germany)

Seven-time World Champion (**1994, 1995, 2000–2004**). Schumacher rebuilt Ferrari from drought to dominance. His **91** career wins stood as the record until Hamilton surpassed it. Comeback with Mercedes 2010–2012 preceded Hamilton's era at the same team.

## Lewis Hamilton (United Kingdom)

**Seven** championships (**2008, 2014–2015, 2017–2020**), tying Schumacher's record. Hamilton holds the record for most pole positions and podiums in F1 history. He won six drivers' titles with Mercedes in the hybrid era.

## Max Verstappen (Netherlands)

Champion **2021, 2022, 2023, 2024** with Red Bull. Youngest race starter and winner in F1 history. His 2021 Abu Dhabi title duel with Hamilton ended controversially under safety-car restart rules.

## Fernando Alonso (Spain)

Two-time champion (**2005, 2006**) with Renault. Known for exceptional racecraft and defensive driving. Returned with Aston Martin after Alpine stint; still competitive into his forties.

## Statistical Notes

Most wins: Hamilton (**103+**). Most poles: Hamilton. Youngest champion: Sebastian Vettel (2010, age 23). Only female points scorer in modern era discussions often cite Lella Lombardi (1975 Spanish GP, half point).

Driver numbers became permanent career choices after 2014 — Verstappen **33** became **1** as champion.
