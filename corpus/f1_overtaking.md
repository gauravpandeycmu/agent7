# Overtaking, DRS, and dirty air in Formula One

Overtaking in modern Formula One is limited less by engine power than by **dirty air**: the turbulent wake behind a leading car reduces downforce on the follower, especially on the front axle. The 2022 ground-effect reset tried to make the wake less damaging so cars could follow closer through medium-speed corners.

## DRS as a sporting patch

The drag reduction system opens an adjustable rear-wing flap in specified zones. It is a sporting bandage on an aerodynamic problem. When DRS-on delta is huge (Baku, Monza), overtaking can look trivial. When the zone is short or the detection point is poorly placed, trains of cars form.

DRS is not available in the first lap of the race (typical rule) and not while the safety car is deployed. Detecting "within one second" at a loop in the tarmac is an electronic sporting rule, not a physics one.

## Slipstream versus downforce

On long straights, a follower benefits from slipstream (reduced drag) even while losing downforce in corners. Monza and Baku reward slipstream; Hungary and Monaco reward mechanical grip and qualifying position because you cannot follow closely enough to slipstream past.

## Defensive driving

The sporting code generally allows a driver to choose a line but not to move twice in reaction. "Opening then closing" on a straight is often penalized. Late blocking under braking is judged as causing a collision.

## Tyre offset as an overtaking tool

A driver on fresher or softer tyres can overtake even without DRS if the compound delta exceeds the dirty-air loss. This is why an undercut that fails to jump a car still sometimes wins the following lap on the main straight.

## 2022–2024 following behavior

Ground-effect floors reduced some of the wake problem but introduced vertical bouncing at stiff ride heights. Teams that ran higher to avoid bouncing often lost peak downforce and could be passed more easily. Technical directives on floor stiffness changed the competitive order mid-2022.
