# Formula One sporting and technical regulations (selected)

The FIA International Sporting Code plus the Formula One Sporting Regulations and Technical Regulations define what a legal car and a legal race weekend look like. Students using this corpus should treat the following as a conceptual map, not a substitute for the current PDF.

## Points and the championships

The drivers' championship and constructors' championship run in parallel. Points for a full-length Grand Prix have used the 25–18–15–12–10–8–6–4–2–1 system for top ten finishers in recent seasons, with occasional extra points for fastest lap when the finisher is also in the top ten. Sprint weekends add a shorter Saturday race with a smaller points scale.

A driver who does not start scores nothing. Classification usually requires completing 90% of the winner's distance; otherwise the result may be "not classified."

## Parc fermé

After qualifying, cars enter parc fermé. Setup changes are tightly limited until the race. Tyre choice, fuel load (in the refuelling-ban era there is no race refuelling), and software maps still offer strategic freedom. Breaking parc fermé without permission typically means starting from the pit lane.

## Safety car, VSC, and red flags

The safety car bunches the field behind the leader. Lapped cars may be allowed to unlap depending on the year's rules. A virtual safety car (VSC) requires drivers to stay above a minimum delta time without a physical safety car on track. Red flags stop the session; restart procedures have changed (standing restart versus rolling) and are a common source of controversy.

## Power unit elements and penalties

Each driver has a limited allocation of internal combustion engines, turbochargers, MGU-H, MGU-K, energy stores, and control electronics. Exceeding the allocation adds grid penalties. Gearbox allocations are separate. This is why a driver may take a 10-place drop at a power circuit to "take the medicine" and start clean for a stretch of races.

## Aerodynamic legality

The technical regulations define reference planes, plank wear, floor edges, and wing flexibility tests. The FIA can issue technical directives mid-season when a concept is judged to violate the intent (flexible rear wings, porpoising-related floor oscillation, ride-height maps that lower the car at speed).

DRS (drag reduction system) may be used in designated zones when a driver is within one second of the car ahead at the detection point, except on the first lap and under safety-car conditions as specified.

## Stewarding

Stewards apply time penalties, drive-throughs, stop-go penalties, grid drops, and points on the super licence. Collisions are judged with guidelines on "who owned the corner." Track-limits deletions of lap times have defined several qualifying sessions at Austria and elsewhere.
