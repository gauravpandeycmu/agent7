# Formula One calendar and circuit notes (race-by-race)

This note is a retrieval corpus for calendar and venue facts. Distances and lap counts change; treat year-specific numbers as typical recent-era figures.

**Bahrain** (Sakhir). Night race under lights. Heavy traction zones punish rear tyres. Often the season opener. Desert wind can swing brake and tyre temperatures between sessions.

**Saudi Arabia** (Jeddah). Fast street circuit with walls. High average speed. Safety-car probability is material for strategy. Overtaking better than Monaco but worse than Bahrain.

**Australia** (Albert Park, Melbourne). Revised layout in the 2020s added overtaking zones. Cooler autumn weather for a March date. Lakeside parkland, not a street circuit in the Monaco sense.

**Japan** (Suzuka). Figure-eight. 130R and Spoon are high-commitment. Historically a title-decider venue. Ferrari versus McLaren lore in the 1980s–2000s.

**China** (Shanghai). Long back straight into a hairpin. DRS trains possible. The circuit returned after a pandemic gap.

**Miami**. Purpose-built around Hard Rock Stadium parking lots. Bumpy. Sprint format has appeared. Heat and thunderstorms.

**Emilia-Romagna** (Imola). Narrow, old-school. Limited overtaking. Varied weather in spring. Tamburello history is a safety landmark.

**Monaco**. Slowest average speed, hardest to pass. Qualifying is the race. Harbour, tunnel, swimming pool complex. Rain is chaos.

**Spain** (Barcelona-Catalunya). Traditional test venue; high-speed last sector. 2020s layout tweaks tried to help passing. Heavy front-left wear.

**Canada** (Montreal, Circuit Gilles Villeneuve). Wall of Champions. Power-sensitive. Weather swings. Long back straight to the hairpin.

**Austria** (Red Bull Ring, Spielberg). Short lap, three DRS zones in some years. Altitude. Track-limits controversy at turns 9–10.

**Britain** (Silverstone). Maggotts-Becketts. High downforce and high speed. Home crowd for several drivers. Copse crash history (2021) is sporting lore.

**Hungary** (Hungaroring). Tight, processional unless weather or strategy intervenes. High downforce. Often a one-stop.

**Belgium** (Spa-Francorchamps). Longest lap in the championship. Eau Rouge / Raidillon. Microclimates: rain at one end, dry at the other. 2021 farce of a three-lap points race is a sporting scar.

**Netherlands** (Zandvoort). Banked turn 3. Orange home crowd. Hard to pass despite banking.

**Italy** (Monza). Temple of speed. Lowest downforce. Slipstream. Tifosi. Parabolica.

**Azerbaijan** (Baku). Longest straight on the calendar in several seasons. Castle section is slow and wall-lined. High safety-car odds.

**Singapore** (Marina Bay). Night street race. High humidity, high brake wear, high downforce. One of the most physically demanding races.

**United States** (Austin, COTA). Combination of Maggotts-like esses and a long back straight. Multiple DRS. Stadium section.

**Mexico** (Mexico City). High altitude: less cooling, less drag, engines and wings in unusual windows. Foro Sol stadium.

**Brazil** (Interlagos, São Paulo). Anti-clockwise. Weather. Senna S. Title-deciding lore (2008 Hamilton, 2021 rain).

**Las Vegas**. Strip night race. Long straights, cold desert night tyres. New on the 2020s calendar.

**Qatar** (Lusail). Night race. Abrasive. Tyre-limit controversies in the first editions. Desert.

**Abu Dhabi** (Yas Marina). Season finale in many years. 2021 safety-car finish remains the most disputed title ending of the hybrid era. Twilight to night.

**Monza versus Monaco** is the calendar's overtaking contrast: one is slipstream, the other is qualifying. **Spa versus Hungaroring** is the weather-versus-processional contrast.

Additional flyaways have included Korea, India, Turkey (Istanbul Park's turn 8), Malaysia (Sepang storms), France (Paul Ricard), Germany (Hockenheim and Nürburgring), and Portugal (Portimão) as rotating or former venues.
