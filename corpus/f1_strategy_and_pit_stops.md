# Formula One Strategy and Pit Stops

The **undercut** is pitting before a rival onto fresh tyres so you jump them when they stop. The **overcut** is staying out longer in clean air and pitting later. Those two pit-window moves are the core of a Grand Prix strategy fight.

Modern F1 strategy also revolves around **tire compounds**, **pit stop timing**, and **safety car** opportunities.

## Tire Compounds

Pirelli supplies **C1–C5** hardness range (formerly soft/medium/hard naming per weekend). Drivers must use at least two compounds in dry races unless wet tires used. **Soft** grips most but degrades fastest; **hard** lasts longest with less pace.

## Undercut and Overcut

**Undercut**: pit before rival, use fresh tire pace to gain time while they stay out on worn rubber, then emerge ahead after their stop.

**Overcut**: stay out longer on clean air while rival pits, set fast laps on warm tires, pit later and jump them.

## Pit Stop Performance

Top teams complete stops under **2.5 seconds** — Red Bull set sub-**1.9s** records. Slow stops cost positions irreversibly at Monaco.

## Safety Car Strategy

**Safety Car** freezes gaps approximately and reduces pit-lane time loss. Teams pit for cheap tire changes. **2021 Abu Dhabi** title finale showed how SC timing can decide championships.

## Sprint Races

**Sprint weekends** award points Saturday; grid set by sprint result Sunday. Alters tire allocation and risk tolerance.

## Fuel and Lift-and-Coast

Hybrid era ended refueling (banned **2010**). Fuel saving and **lift-and-coast** manage flow limits. Engineers optimize **minimum lap time** vs tire degradation curves.

## Two-Stop vs One-Stop

Track abrasion and temperature determine viability. High-degradation tracks (Barcelona, Bahrain outer) favor two stops; low-degradation (Paul Ricard historically) enable one-stop.

Strategy errors — pitting under VSC too late, wrong compound, crossing white line in pit entry — cost championships as much as raw pace.
