# Formula One Hybrid Power Units (2014–present)

Since **2014**, Formula One has used **1.6-litre V6 turbocharged** hybrid power units. The regulations cap fuel flow and per-race fuel allowance, forcing efficiency alongside power.

## Energy Recovery Systems

Two motor-generator units define the hybrid package:

- **MGU-K** (Motor Generator Unit – Kinetic): harvests energy under braking, deploys up to **120 kW** for acceleration. Drivers manage deployment via steering-wheel maps.
- **MGU-H** (Motor Generator Unit – Heat): recovers energy from the turbocharger's spinning shaft, reducing turbo lag and feeding the battery or MGU-K.

The **ES** (energy store) battery must weigh at least **20 kg**. Total system power exceeds 1000 horsepower when ICE and electrical deployment combine.

## Manufacturers

Mercedes, Ferrari, Honda (now Red Bull Powertrains), and Renault (Alpine) have supplied units. Mercedes' **PU106** family dominated early hybrid years due to split-turbo packaging and superior thermal management.

## 2022 Aero Reset and Porpoising

The **2022** rules moved downforce generation to **ground effect** underfloor tunnels, banning complex bargeboards. Teams ran cars very stiff to maximize aerodynamic sensitivity.

**Porpoising** emerged: cars bounced vertically (drivers described bouncing up and down) as downforce loaded and unloaded cyclically at speed. The floor stalled, then bit again. The floor flexed and airflow stalled. Drivers reported back pain; Lewis Hamilton struggled visibly at the 2022 Azerbaijan Grand Prix.

The FIA introduced **technical directives** mid-2022 limiting floor flexibility and raising ride height. By 2023 most teams mitigated porpoising through floor stiffness and setup compromises.

## Fuel and Oil

Fuel must be **E10** (10% sustainable ethanol). Oil burn limits prevent using engine oil as fuel. Substances like **triol** in fuel blends have triggered regulatory scrutiny and occasional disqualifications.

## Reliability vs Performance

Teams balance **grid penalties** for exceeding power-unit component allocations. Each driver gets limited MGU-K, MGU-H, turbo, ICE, and battery sets per season. Strategic penalties affect championship fights late in the year.

## Future Direction

Regulations target **100% sustainable fuels** and simplified hybrid architecture for 2026, with increased electrical power fraction and removal of MGU-H in the published framework discussions.

Hybrid F1 proves that road-relevant efficiency technology can coexist with 350+ km/h top speeds when regulations incentivize both.
